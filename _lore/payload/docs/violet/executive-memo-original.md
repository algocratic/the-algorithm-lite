MEMORANDUM
Responsible Use of AI, Revisited

TO: Executive Council
FROM: Computer Science Faculty - teacherbot.help
DATE: July 30, 2026

Why this memo exists - exempt from condensation
Current policy frames AI risk as tool error. That is the wrong frame.
The real risks come from the tool working as intended.
Risk one: the tool says yes. This harms students.
Risk two: our own revisions delete our reasons. This harms the institution.
We caught our process deleting its reason twice during the experiment. This memo is the second catch.

Finding 1 - Unlimited help produces a person who asked
A frontier AI system says yes to every request.
A student who asks for the gap closed receives a closed gap. The student learns nothing.
Integrity policy targets plagiarism. Nothing was stolen. The struggle was lost.
Fix: scope AI access per assignment, in the syllabus, as a lab states safety rules.
The discipline belongs to the course, not the tool.

Finding 2 - Our documents drift under our own maintenance
Revisers already know why each policy exists.
To a reader who knows, the reason reads as ornament.
Each careful cut is defensible. The sum deletes the reason.
Three practices prevent this. All three are cheap:

Mark the rationale section of each governing document exempt from condensation. Write the exemption inside the section.
Keep every cut. Revision history is the recovery path, not clutter.
Assign one reviewer per cycle who does not know the answer. The reason is visible only to a reader who needs it.

Supporting practices
No AI output becomes a decision until a named person approves it.
Approval must cost a signature, not a setting.
Prefer evaluators that cannot be flattered: test suites, blind rubrics, compilers.

Recommendation - one-semester pilot, two programs
Add tiered AI-access statements to syllabi.
Add rationale-exemption language to one governing document as a template.
Require a named approver for AI-assisted administrative output.
I will supply the experiment's records: full revision history, drift log, and the bug we caught our own process committing.
We kept that bug in the record on purpose. That is responsible use, revisited.
