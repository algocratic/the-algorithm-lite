# Executive Memo Summary

Status: draft
Source: issue #4, executive memo ingestion

This memo argues that the main risks of AI are not tool failures but tool successes. When AI works as intended, it can remove the struggle that students need for learning and it can quietly erase the reasons behind institutional policy during revisions.

Key points:

- Current AI policy focuses too much on tool error. The bigger risk is that AI can solve problems for students before they learn how to solve them.
- Unlimited AI help can produce a finished answer instead of a learned outcome. That is a loss of development, not a theft of content.
- Policy revisions can remove the rationale that explains why a rule exists. Small cuts can add up to a policy that no longer reflects its purpose.
- The memo recommends three safeguards:
  - mark rationale sections as exempt from condensation;
  - preserve revision history;
  - use at least one reviewer who does not already know the answer.
- The memo also recommends human approval for AI-assisted administrative output, clear approval steps, and objective evaluators such as tests, rubrics, and compilers.
- The recommended pilot is a one-semester test across two programs with tiered AI-use statements in syllabi and rationale-protection language in a governing document.
